How it works

From a photo at the polling unit to a public record.

Five steps.Scroll to light each one

  1. Photograph or report the posted result sheet

    When counting ends, the result sheet is posted at the polling unit. Photograph or report the sheet only, never people.

    If Form EC8A is not available: other evidence, such as a video, handwritten numbers or a photo of the results board, is accepted only after a verifier has audited it, and it is marked as lower-confidence.

  2. Share it and tag Binchika

    Send the photo by any channel. Your handle becomes a one-way code straight away, so we never store who you are.

  3. Read the numbers

    Open-source text recognition reads each party's votes. Anything the machine is unsure of goes to a person to check.

  4. Compare every source

    When at least 85% of independent reports from a unit agree, the tally is high confidence. It is then compared with INEC's IReV upload. Any mismatch is flagged, with its sources and reason.

  5. Publish for anyone to check

    Only high-agreement tallies are written to the hash-chained public ledger. Altering any entry breaks the chain, and anyone can see that it broke. Only INEC declares results.

What we can't tell you (yet)

INEC does not publish turnout or party support by age or sex. If Binchika shows such figures on election day, they come from citizen reports and are labelled crowdsourced, never presented as official.

Neutrality pledge

No party gets a louder voice.

No advertising, ever

No party ads, sponsored posts or paid placement. No money tied to any party, candidate or affiliate.

Equal space for all parties

All 22 INEC-registered parties get identical cards, shuffled on every visit. Colours are assigned alphabetically and carry no meaning.

Anyone can audit it

The code, the confidence rules and every processed tally are public. If we get something wrong, you can prove it.
