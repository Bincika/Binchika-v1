Volunteer verifiers · unpaid · anonymous by default

Check the count, not the candidate.

Verifiers look at the citizen photos coming in from their own state and flag anything that doesn't add up. They never change a result. A flag goes to a Regional Verifier, then to an automated re-investigation. No single person can change a result: a correction needs three separate seats, and every decision is published in the ledger.

How a problem gets fixed

  1. Citizens report
    When reporting opens, photos or reports of the posted result sheet will be accepted on the channels announced here. Reporting is not open during readiness testing.
  2. People confirm the reading
    A machine may propose the numbers; they count only after a verifier confirms them beside the photo. High confidence needs at least 85% agreement among at least 3 independent confirmed readings.
  3. Verifiers flag
    An SZV or NRV sees something wrong in their area and raises a flag with a reason.
  4. AI re-investigates
    All photos and reports for that unit are re-read and compared, and a summary is written.
  5. Consensus-Approved Corrections
    No single person can change a result. A correction needs three separate seats: the verifier who flags it, a Regional Verifier who escalates it, and the BinchikAdmin, who records it after the AI re-check. Approved fixes are new ledger entries, so nothing is erased. (Planned: a quorum of at least 5 of 7 randomly assigned Regional Verifiers.)

If Form EC8A is not available: other evidence, such as a video, handwritten numbers or a photo of the results board, is accepted only after a verifier has audited it, and it is marked as lower-confidence. Auditing such evidence is part of a verifier's work.

Citizens photograph or report the posted result sheet. Binchika compares those reports with INEC's IReV uploads and writes high-agreement tallies to a public hash-chained ledger. Only INEC declares results.

Three roles

Citizen monitors

Anyone at a polling unit. Photograph or report the posted result sheet and send it in. You need no account, you stay independent, and you can call out any mistake, including ours.

129

State Zone Verifiers (SZV)

Three for most states and three for the FCT. The states with the largest share of INEC's 2023 register have more: seven for Lagos and Kano, five for Kaduna, Rivers, Katsina, Oyo and Delta. Always an odd number, so a state vote can never tie. They see incoming reports for their own state only and flag problems.

24

Regional Verifiers (NRV)

Three for each of eight regions (draft): an odd number, so a regional vote can never tie. They oversee the SZVs in their region, review escalations, and can flag in any state of their region.

Every verifier is known publicly only by a sequential 5-character ID (for example V0042); only the BinchikAdmin can link it to a person. The chain is SZV → regional NRV → OpsAdmin → BinchikAdmin, and a correction needs three separate seats: the verifier who flags it, a different Regional Verifier who escalates it, and the BinchikAdmin, who records it. Who does what

Every seat is fixed in number. A flag travels up the chain, from SZV to BinchikAdmin. No single seat can change a result: a correction needs the verifier who flags it, a different Regional Verifier and the BinchikAdmin.

  1. 1 seat

    BinchikAdmin

    The only superadmin. It is locked to one owner address that can never be changed or handed on, and it needs an authenticator app to act.

    • Records or rejects corrections that a different Regional Verifier has escalated, never on a flag it raised itself (each correction is a new public ledger entry)
    • Invites verifiers, releases seats and appoints the three OpsAdmins

  2. Exactly 3 seats · OPS-1 OPS-2 OPS-3

    OpsAdmin

    Appointed only by the BinchikAdmin, never by code or application, and an authenticator app is required.

    • Runs the AI re-investigation and helps with reviews
    • Cannot approve corrections, invite, release seats or see who holds a seat

  3. 24 seats · 3 per region · 8 regions

    Regional Verifier (NRV)

    Reviews flags from the states in its region, then escalates them with findings or dismisses them. Three per region, so a regional vote can't tie.

  4. 129 seats · 3, 5 or 7 per state and the FCT

    State Zone Verifier (SZV)

    Reads citizen reports from its own state and raises flags with a reason someone else can check. Never edits a result.

Roles never change. A seat's role, its account and its email address are fixed for as long as the seat is held. No one can be promoted, and no one can ever become BinchikAdmin. To change who holds a seat, the BinchikAdmin releases it (the old public ID is retired for good) and invites or appoints someone new. The database enforces all of this, not just the website.

The rules, enforced by the database

Your state, your reports

You choose your state of origin or of residence when you apply, and it can't be changed later. Someone from Lagos verifies Lagos, not Bauchi. The database rejects any other assignment.

24 hours, then 6

First-wave selectees get a one-time code and 24 hours to create an account and complete their profile. An unused place passes to the next most compatible applicant from the same state, who gets 6 hours.

Flags, not edits

Verifiers cannot write or change results. They can read reports in their area and raise flags. A correction needs three separate seats (the verifier who flags it, a different Regional Verifier and the BinchikAdmin), after AI re-investigation.

Unpaid, on purpose

This is volunteer work. Expecting payment rules an applicant out, which protects verifiers from anyone trying to buy influence.

    Selection timetable

    Fair, published and on time.

    Where we are now: applications are not open yet. They open only after our privacy notice has been reviewed and published. Every date below moves with that, except two fixed points: a holiday pause from 20 December to 3 January (no deadlines, no offers, no training) and the election days themselves, 16 January and 6 February 2027.
    1. Privacy notice published. Applications cannot open before this.
    2. Application window: two weeks, 2–16 November 2026 (proposed). It opens only if the privacy notice is in force by then. Any change is announced here and on @getbinchika. Late applications are never reviewed.
    3. Screening: 17 November – 4 December (proposed), about two and a half weeks. The same published questions for everyone, a bias filter, and a spread check across states. The scoring code is open source.
    4. First wave, early December. Each selectee receives a seat code by email only, from noreply@binchika.com.
    5. 24 hours to claim. Claim your seat at binchika.com/join with an email and password, then confirm your email and sign the verifier pledge. Places not taken pass to the next eligible applicant from the same state, who has 6 hours.
    6. Training block 1, mid-December (online, evenings): duties, privacy, and how to flag.
    7. 20 December – 3 January: holiday pause. Nothing is due. Any place still open is re-offered from 4 January.
    8. Training block 2 and a full dry run, 4–10 January.
    9. 16 January and 6 February 2027: election days. Then 72 hours of active verifying after each.

    The detailed proposed dates are on the application page. If the privacy notice is late, the window and screening shrink; the holiday pause and the election days do not move.
