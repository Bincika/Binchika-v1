      Voter register · 2023 and 2027 · published figures

      The register behind the count

      Every result on this page will be read against INEC's register of voters. This section compares the preliminary register for the 2027 elections with the 2023 register, using published figures only, and lists the questions those figures raise.

      Figures from published sources, not an official INEC table. The 2027 figures are INEC's announcement of 6 October 2026 as reported by the press. They are preliminary and can change before the final register, due on 15 December 2026. No names or individual records are shown. To check your own entry with INEC, see how to check your name; the zone totals also have their own page.

      The register grew by 812,714 fewer than the number of new registrations. That gap is consistent with entries removed by the clean-up, for example duplicates or deceased voters, or with new registrations that did not pass the final biometric checks. INEC has not published a breakdown, so this is arithmetic, not an INEC figure.

      By geopolitical zone

      {{REGISTER_ZONE_TABLE}}

      Every zone grew. The North-West grew most, by 2,827,849 (+12.7%), and remains the largest zone. The South-West grew least in percentage terms (+9.2%), and the South-East least in numbers (+1,068,612). The six zones add up exactly to the national total; this page is not built if they do not.

      Open questions for INEC

      Binchika is non-partisan. These questions are about the process, not about any party or candidate. Each rests on a published statement, linked beneath it. INEC's answers will be added here, with their source, when they are published.

      1. Online publication. Section 19(1) of the Electoral Act 2026 requires the register to be displayed at every registration area and on INEC's website. INEC displayed it at registration areas from 9 to 15 October 2026. FIJ found INEC's register portal still showing "There are currently no preliminary registers to display" on 18 October. INEC says the same register was published online on 26 October. A presidential campaign has also asked INEC to explain conflicting statements about the display dates.

        Questions: When exactly was the register first available online? Does the 14-day period for claims and objections in Section 19(2) run from the online publication? Will INEC publish the dates and access records for its portal?

        Sources: Leadership (6 Oct), FIJ (18 Oct), Legit.ng (22 Oct), Vanguard (27 Oct), ThisDay (28 Oct).

      2. Questionable records and suspected AI-generated photographs. INEC says "a number of questionable registration records" were flagged during the physical display and are being reviewed. It says it is examining the suspicious photographs in the register published online, "including possible technical or procedural lapses", and that the online register "does not reflect all ongoing data-cleanup activities".

        Questions: How many records were flagged, and in which states? How did such photographs pass photo capture and biometric checks? Will INEC publish what it found, and how many records it removed, before the final register?

        Sources: Vanguard (27 Oct), ThisDay (28 Oct).

      3. Deceased and ineligible voters. In December 2025, INEC's chairman described the register as "unclean" and asked civil society and community leaders to help clean it. In June 2026, an INEC official told Vanguard that there are thousands of deceased people on the register, and that they can be removed only through claims and objections with supporting evidence.

        Questions: How many entries did the final biometric clean-up remove, and for which reasons (duplicates, deaths, under-age registration)? How many claims and objections were received and upheld, nationally and by state? What explains the 812,714 gap between new registrations and net growth?

        Sources: BusinessDay (12 Dec 2025), Vanguard (June 2026).

      4. State figures. State totals were announced at a press briefing but are not on INEC's website, and press versions differ (Benue; a misprinted FCT figure in one report).

        Question: Will INEC publish its own state, local government and ward totals now, rather than only with the final register?

      5. Age profile. Reported figures put 18–34-year-olds at 28.79% of the preliminary register, against 39.65% reported for 2023.

        Question: Can INEC publish the age breakdown of both registers on the same basis, so the change can be checked?

      6. Readiness for 2027. INEC began collection of newly issued voter cards (PVCs) on 9 October 2026. The final register is due on 15 December 2026. FIJ described 18 October as 90 days before the election, which puts polling about a month after the final register.

        Questions: How will voters and parties inspect the final register before polling day? How many new PVCs have been collected so far? Will INEC publish collection figures by state?

        Sources: Leadership (6 Oct), FIJ (18 Oct).
