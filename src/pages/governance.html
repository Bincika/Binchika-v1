AI data governance

AI suggests. Rules and people decide.

Nothing an AI produces reaches the public ledger without passing the same published rules as everything else, and every correction needs three separate human seats: the verifier who flags it, a Regional Verifier who escalates it, and the BinchikAdmin, who records it.

Limits we design around

  • Handwriting, low light, folds and stamps cause misreads, so every low-confidence field goes to a person.
  • AI cannot spot a doctored sheet on its own. Independent reporters agreeing, comparison with IReV and the pre-election baselines can identify document inconsistencies; they do not determine fraud or legal validity.
  • Language models can invent text. Summaries must quote and cite, and any unsupported sentence is removed.
  • Error rates are monitored by state; if one region misreads more, it goes to people.

The AI constitution

  1. Suggest, never decide.
  2. Label everything. Every AI output is marked and logged with the model, version, input hash and time.
  3. No personal data. Hashed handles, stripped photo metadata and blurred faces before any model sees an image. Nothing identifiable goes to outside AI services.
  4. Rules beat models. If they disagree, the case goes to a person.
  5. Say how sure it is. Below 0.9 confidence per field, a person confirms.
  6. No politics. No predictions, no persuasion, and the same pipeline for every party.
  7. Auditable. Anyone can ask to re-run an AI step on the public evidence.
  8. Least power. AI writes only to a review queue, never to the ledger or the results.
  9. Kill switch. Any AI step can be turned off and the system keeps working with people.
  10. Consensus-Approved Corrections. No single person can change a result. A correction needs the verifier who flags it, a different Regional Verifier who escalates it, and the BinchikAdmin, who records it after the AI re-check. It becomes a new public ledger entry, so nothing is erased.
